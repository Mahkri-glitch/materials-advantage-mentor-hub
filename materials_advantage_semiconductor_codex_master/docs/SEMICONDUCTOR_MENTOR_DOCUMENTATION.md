# Semiconductor Mentor Documentation and Resources

## Purpose
This documentation uses semiconductors as one example pathway for Materials Science students at UCF. It is based on Maahir's experience learning about semiconductor-related work through research, coursework, networking, and mentoring.

## Important framing
This is not the only Materials Science path. It is one backbone that can show students how to explore a technical field, build skills, and decide whether it interests them.

## Core student questions
- What is semiconductor process engineering?
- How do materials students fit into semiconductors?
- What is the difference between process, device, design, packaging, photonics, quantum, and data roles?
- What UCF resources can help me explore this?
- What classes and skills matter?
- How do I use research to break into this field?
- How do conferences and networking help?

## Banerjee Lab experience section
In Dr. Parag Banerjee’s lab, Maahir was exposed to thin-film process science through ALD, in situ characterization, and materials processing. This helped clarify how semiconductor work depends on surfaces, interfaces, deposition, etching, metrology, process control, and data analysis.

## Suggested student action
Pick one process, one material system, or one measurement method. Learn the basics, find a UCF professor or lab connected to it, and ask a mentor to help plan the next step.
